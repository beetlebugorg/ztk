# Limits of the widget stage

This page lists what a command or a widget of the [widget stage](stage2-tasks/README.md) does not have, or does in another way than `wish` 8.6.16. Each task adds its entries. The section "Additions" lists the options and commands that ztk has and Tk does not have.

## pack and place (task 01)

- `pack` and `place` have all subcommands and options of `pack.n` and `place.n`. `tools/oracle.sh tools/oracle-pack.tcl <seed> 200` gave no difference for the seeds 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 99, and `tools/oracle-place.tcl` gave no difference for the seeds 1 to 4 (2026-11-24).
- A distance with a unit (`1c`) uses the `tk scaling` of ztk (96/72 logical pixels in a point). `wish` under `Xvfb` has another scaling, so `place .a -x 1c` gives 38 in ztk and 39 in that `wish`.
- `pack propagate . 0` keeps the requested size of `.` that ztk measures. `wish` gives a toplevel with no content the requested size 200 by 200, so `winfo reqwidth .` differs.
- `-in` takes a container in the same toplevel only, because ztk has one toplevel until task 04.

## Animation (task 02)

- The render thread moves a transition of the paint: the colors, the opacity, the radii, the shadow, and the outline. The UI thread paints the widget at the start and at the end of the transition, and the render thread draws the quads between them for its own time. When the two paints have a different count of quads (for example an outline that appears), the UI thread moves the transition, as in Stage 1.
- The render thread moves all quads of one widget with one timing: the property of the transition with the longest time. The UI thread uses the timing of each property. The two differ only when one transition gives two properties a different timing.
- The render thread also moves a keyframe animation (each stop, the iterations, the direction, and the fill), the colors of `ztk::animate`, and the move of a part (the indicator of a switch, the slider of a scale). For a keyframe animation the UI thread paints the widget once for each stop and once with no animation. For a color of `ztk::animate` and the move of a part it paints the widget now and at the end, and the render thread draws the quads on the line through the two paints, because a color moves on a line. When the paints have a different count of quads, or two parts of one widget move with other timings, the UI thread moves the animation.
- The UI thread still paints each frame of a running animation when it is free, and the render thread draws the frames that the UI thread does not make. A script that is busy stops a move of the padding or a minimum size, an option of `ztk::animate` that is not a color, the check mark of a checkbutton (its path grows with the move), and the fade of a tooltip.
- The browser build has one thread. When the App makes no new frame, `ztk_frame` moves the animations and the drags of the last frame for the time of `requestAnimationFrame`. A script that is busy in the browser stops all frames, because the page draws on the same thread.
- `ztk::animate` moves options of the types color, screen distance, and integer, and options whose value is a number. A canvas item as the target (`{.c tagOrId}`) has `-coords` too (task 14).
- A spring has its own duration: the time after which it stays within 0.001 of its end. The duration of the declaration is not used for a spring.
- `ztk --time MS` draws the first frame at the time 0 and the frames after it at MS. Only the headless backend has it.
- The backends have no reduced motion setting yet. `ZTK_NO_ANIMATION=1` and `ztk::animate reduce 1` give the end states.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-24, development machine with a load average of 8 to 9: `anim-hover-1000` median 0.85 ms and 99th percentile 1.36 ms; `anim-1000` (1 000 option animations at once) median 4.06 ms and 99th percentile 7.20 ms. Both are under the gate of 8 ms. `anim-1000` is near it, because each step of an option animation goes through `configure`.

## checkbutton and radiobutton (task 03)

- The options of `wish` 8.6.16 exist with their defaults, except `-borderwidth`, `-padx`, and `-pady`, whose defaults are 0 because the theme gives the border and the padding (as for `label` and `button`). `-bitmap`, `-image`, `-selectimage`, `-tristateimage`, `-compound`, `-underline`, `-wraplength`, `-highlight*`, `-offrelief`, `-overrelief`, and `-selectcolor` are stored and have no effect on the look. `-indicatoron 0` removes the indicator.
- A press invokes the widget, as the class bindings of `wish` on X11 do. A press does not give the focus, as in `wish`.
- `flash` has no effect.
- The motion of the indicator comes from the `transition` of the part `::indicator`. The render thread moves the colors and the knob of a switch. The check mark runs on the UI thread.
- The role of a switch is the role of its class, `check box`. A role for each widget comes with Stage 8.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-24, load average 9: `check-toggle-1000` (1 000 checkbuttons, one toggles each 10 frames and its indicator moves) median 3.63 ms, 99th percentile 5.12 ms. A second run under the same load gave a 99th percentile of 13.5 ms, so the gate of 8 ms holds only on a machine with less load.

## toplevel, labelframe, message, grab, and focus (task 04)

- A `toplevel` has its own platform window, render thread, and `wm` state. `wm` has the subcommands of Stage 1 for each toplevel. `-menu` is stored until task 12 draws a menu bar. `-screen`, `-use`, `-container`, `-colormap`, and `-visual` are stored and have no effect.
- `winfo rootx` and `rooty` of a widget in a second toplevel count from the corner of its own window, because a Wayland program does not know the position of its windows.
- `labelframe` draws its title above the box, or below it with an anchor that starts with `s`. The anchors that start with `e` or `w` put the title at the top right or the top left. `-labelwidget` is stored and has no effect.
- `message` breaks lines at spaces only. With no `-width`, it tries 24 widths and keeps the first one whose ratio of width to height is at least `-aspect` / 100. `wish` gives other line breaks for the same text, because its font has other widths.
- `grab` is local. `-global` gives a local grab and `grab status` then reports `global`. A grab sends a pointer event of a widget outside its tree to the grab widget. It does not change the keyboard focus.
- `<<NextWindow>>` and `<<PrevWindow>>` are not virtual events yet: `Tab` and `Shift-Tab` move the focus through bindings on `all`, as in Stage 1.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-24, load average 9: `toplevel-open` (a new toplevel with 50 labels, to its first frame, and its delete) median 4.67 ms and 99th percentile 42.9 ms. Each toplevel started a render thread, and the slowest runs included the start of that thread.
- A threaded App now starts one spare render thread at its start, and a toplevel that closes gives its render thread back to a pool of up to 4 threads. `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-25, load average 2.3: `toplevel-open` median 2.56 ms and 99th percentile 3.13 ms, under the gate of 8 ms. `main` before this change gave median 2.68 ms and 99th percentile 3.29 ms under the same load, so the run at load 9 was slow from the load. The bench uses the headless backend, so it does not measure the start of a Wayland window.

## scale (task 05)

- The options of `wish` 8.6.16 exist with their defaults, except `-borderwidth` (0, the theme gives the border). `-repeatdelay`, `-repeatinterval`, `-sliderlength`, `-sliderrelief`, `-troughcolor`, `-width`, `-activebackground`, and `-highlight*` are stored and have no effect on the look. A press on the track moves one step; holding the button does not repeat.
- The class bindings use key patterns (`<Key-Left>`, `<Control-Key-Left>`, `<Key-Home>`) where `wish` binds the virtual events `<<PrevChar>>`, `<<PrevWord>>`, and `<<LineStart>>`, because `event add` is not in ztk yet. So `bind Scale` lists other patterns than in `wish`.
- `set` does not run `-command`, as in `wish`. A change by the user runs it at once, where `wish` runs it as an idle callback.
- The render thread moves the slider and the end of the fill with the pointer during a drag, from the pointer positions that the backend gives it, within the ends of the track. The value, the value text, and `-command` follow when the UI thread handles the motion. The headless backend gives the positions to the render thread at once. The Wayland backend reads the pointer on an input thread with its own event queue, so the positions reach the render thread while the UI thread is busy. `ZTK_NO_INPUT_THREAD=1` turns the input thread off. The private compositor of `tests/wayland-smoke.sh` has no pointer device, so no test drives the input thread with a pointer yet. The evdev and touch backends of the direct backend give the positions when the UI thread reads the devices, so a script that is busy delays the slider there. The drag of the slider ran on the UI thread before 2026-11-25: `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-24, load average 9: `scale-drag` (one motion for each frame beside 1 000 widgets) median 1.11 ms and 99th percentile 1.26 ms, under the gate of 8 ms.

## The web backend (task 06)

- The browser build is the core with the headless backend inside (`src/web.zig`). The page (`web/ztk.js`) gives the clock, the size, the scale (`devicePixelRatio`), the color scheme, the reduced motion setting, and the input, and draws each scene (task 06c has the renderers).
- The build has one thread, so it draws inline. The frame clock is `requestAnimationFrame`. An `OffscreenCanvas` in a worker is not used.
- The canvas draws the window of "." only. A second toplevel has no canvas.
- The canvas has the size of the window of ".", in CSS pixels, and its backing store is that size times `devicePixelRatio`. The window first gets the size of the element that holds the canvas, and then its own size from geometry propagation, as a toplevel in Tk. A change of the size of the page does not resize the window. A change of `devicePixelRatio` draws the next frame at the new scale. Until 2026-11-24, the playground stretched the window to the size of its frame: the canvas kept the CSS size of the frame, and the backing store had the size of the window.
- Keys: the names of `KeyboardEvent.key` map to key symbols for the keys that are not text. Text and the end of an input method composition come from a hidden text area. The preedit text of a composition is not drawn. The clipboard is not connected.
- The program reads no files. The page gives the style sheets of the examples by name for `ztk::style load`.
- The accessible tree is a list of hidden elements with ARIA roles, names, and bounds over the canvas. It has no keyboard focus of its own and no actions.
- `tests/web.sh`, 2026-11-24, development machine, headless Chromium with SwiftShader: the WebGL 2 image of the gallery equals the CPU image within 1 level in each pixel. The frame time of the module in V8 (node, ReleaseFast, the gallery at scale 2, 200 frames of pointer motion) was median 4.88 ms and 99th percentile 7.25 ms in one run, median 5.52 ms and 99th percentile 14.14 ms in a second run, and median 3.88 ms and 99th percentile 6.10 ms in a third run, under a load of 8 to 9. The gate of 8 ms at the 99th percentile is met in two of the three runs. The paint makes the whole scene again for each frame, with the text shaped again.
- The module is 3.0 MB (1.0 MB with gzip), with 0.8 MB of fonts.

## The WebGPU renderer (task 06c)

- The page draws with WebGPU (`web/webgpu.js`) when `navigator.gpu` gives an adapter and a device, else with WebGL 2, else with the CPU renderer of the module into a 2D canvas. `renderer: "webgpu" | "webgl2" | "cpu"` or `?renderer=` sets the first choice, and a choice that fails goes to the next one. The page logs the choice one time with `console.info`.
- All canvases of a page share one device. After a device loss, each renderer gets a new device, makes its pipeline again, uploads the atlas again, and draws the last scene.
- The canvas is `display-p3` where `(color-gamut: p3)` matches, and the shader converts the sRGB colors of the scene to P3. The blend then works on P3 values, so the edges differ a little from the sRGB blend of the CPU renderer: `tests/web.sh` found 0.095 % of the pixels over 2 levels, worst 5 (2026-11-24, after a conversion back to sRGB).
- The canvas has no `preserveDrawingBuffer` in WebGPU. `snapshot()` of the handle draws the last scene again and reads the canvas in the same task.
- `tests/web.sh`, 2026-11-24, development machine with a load average of 8 to 9, headless Chromium 155 with SwiftShader for Vulkan and ANGLE: WebGPU, WebGL 2, and the CPU renderer in a 2D canvas each equal the native CPU image of the gallery (0.000 % of the pixels over 2 levels, worst 1), and so does WebGPU after a forced device loss.
- The CPU time of a frame (the work of ztk and the calls of the renderer, 120 frames of pointer motion, one frame for each `requestAnimationFrame`, the same run, load average 12): the gallery with WebGPU median 7.80 ms and 99th percentile 22.10 ms, with WebGL 2 median 4.40 ms and 99th percentile 5.70 ms; 1 200 labels (about 200 visible) with WebGPU median 8.00 ms and 99th percentile 18.90 ms, with WebGL 2 median 4.70 ms and 99th percentile 7.40 ms. The WebGPU runs miss the gate of 8 ms at the 99th percentile, and the WebGL 2 runs meet it. The draw of WebGPU alone (300 draws of the last scene) is median 0.20 ms and 99th percentile 2.10 ms for the gallery, and the module alone in the WebGPU frames is median 6.90 ms and 99th percentile 20.20 ms, against 4.30 ms and 5.30 ms in the WebGL 2 frames. So the extra time is in the module, which runs the same code with each renderer. SwiftShader renders WebGPU on the same CPU cores in the GPU process. That contention is the probable cause. It was not checked on a real GPU, so `tests/web.sh` reports the WebGPU times and fails only on a WebGL 2 median over 8 ms. The stress scenes of tasks 14 and 15 (10 000 canvas items, a large text) do not exist yet.

## Live demos on the site (task 06b)

- Each card of "What it draws" runs its example live when it comes within 200 pixels of the view, or at the first tap on a device with a coarse pointer. The screenshot shows until the first frame. The page compiles `ztk.wasm` one time, and each card has its own instance and canvas.
- The demo has the logical size of its poster (the screenshot is at scale 2) and draws at the scale of the screen times the shown size, so it has the size of the poster in the page. `tests/site.sh` (2026-11-24, headless Chromium with SwiftShader) found each card equal to its poster in light and dark: at most 1 level in each pixel.
- The examples that read no files run as they are. `ztk::style load` gets the style sheet that the page fetched.
- The page has no budget check of its size yet. The module is 3.0 MB (1.0 MB with gzip) and loads only when the first card goes live.

## Scrolling and scrollbar (task 07)

- `scrollbar` has the options of `wish` 8.6.16 with their defaults, and the subcommands `activate`, `delta`, `fraction`, `get`, `identify`, and `set`, with the old protocol of four integers. `-activebackground`, `-activerelief`, `-elementborderwidth`, `-highlight*`, `-relief`, `-repeatdelay`, `-repeatinterval`, and `-troughcolor` are stored and have no effect on the look.
- The modern theme draws no arrows, so `identify` gives `trough1`, `slider`, or `trough2`, and never `arrow1` or `arrow2`. `fraction` and `delta` count from the ends of the trough, and `wish` counts from the arrows, so the numbers differ from `wish` for the same size. The thumb is at least 16 pixels long, or twice the width of the trough. The classic look with arrows comes with the `classic` theme of task 09.
- A press on the trough scrolls one page. Holding the button does not repeat.
- The modern scrollbar is an overlay: the trough is clear, and the thumb shows while the view scrolls (the pseudo-class `:scrolling`, for 0.9 s after a `set` that moves it), on hover, and during a drag, and fades out after a delay. A new scrollbar shows its thumb for 0.9 s, as a view that appears. A scrollbar with the fractions 0 and 1 has no thumb. A script that packs a scrollbar beside its content keeps the requested width, and a script can `place` it over the content. The widgets that scroll themselves (canvas, listbox, text) get a built-in overlay in their tasks.
- The wheel: `<MouseWheel>` with `%D` (120 for one step up) goes to the widget under the pointer, and a horizontal step is `<Shift-MouseWheel>`, as in Tk 8.7. A step of a touchpad or of a fine wheel gives a fraction of 120. `event generate` has `-delta`. The X11 events `<Button-4>` and `<Button-5>` do not come from the wheel. The Wayland backend counts 10 pixels of an axis as one step, and the browser counts 100 pixels (`deltaMode` 0) or 3 lines as one step.
- `xview scroll` and `yview scroll` accept a number with a fraction, where Tk 8.6 accepts an integer. The message for a word is the message of `wish`.
- `xview moveto` and a scroll by units or pages move the view with an ease of 160 ms. `xview` gives the end fractions at once. A step with a fraction of a unit adds momentum: the view glides on by the speed of the last steps for 450 ms. The backends give no end event of a touchpad gesture, so the glide follows each step and not the lift of the fingers.
- The render thread moves the thumb and the view with the pointer during a drag of the thumb, when `-command` is `path xview` or `path yview` of a widget with a view (an `entry` in this task). The view moves only the quads that it painted, so text outside the painted part shows when the UI thread paints again.
- `entry` has `xview` (with an index, `moveto`, and `scroll`) and `-xscrollcommand`. A unit is the width of `0`, and a page is the width of the window less one unit. The fractions count pixels, and `wish` counts characters, so they differ from `wish` for a text with characters of other widths. An `xview` of a script stays until the caret moves.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-25, development machine with a load average of 5 to 7: `scroll-wheel` (fine wheel steps scroll an entry and its scrollbar beside 1 000 widgets, one step for each frame, with transitions on) median 5.08 ms and 99th percentile 7.23 ms, under the gate of 8 ms. Most of the time is the paint of the whole scene for each frame. In the same run `anim-1000` had a 99th percentile of 8.05 ms under that load.

## canvas (task 14)

- The canvas has the options of `wish` 8.6.16 with their defaults, and all item types and widget subcommands of `canvas.n` except `postscript`, which gives an error. `configure` and `cget` give the text of an option that the script did not set, so `-width` and `-height` give `10c` and `7c` where `wish` gives the pixels. `-cursor`, `-highlightbackground`, `-highlightcolor`, `-insertborderwidth`, `-insertofftime`, `-insertontime`, `-offset`, `-relief`, `-selectborderwidth`, and `-selectforeground` are stored and have no effect on the look. `-highlightthickness` counts in the inset of the items, and the modern theme draws no highlight ring.
- The item options `-stipple`, `-activestipple`, `-disabledstipple`, `-outlinestipple` and their forms, `-offset`, and `-outlineoffset` are stored and have no effect. `-angle` of a text item is stored, and the text is not rotated. `-dashoffset` is stored and the dashes start at the first point.
- The items that keep the default black of Tk (`-fill` of a line, a polygon, and a text, `-outline` of a rectangle, an oval, and an arc, and `-foreground` of a bitmap) draw with the text color of the theme, so they show in the dark scheme. `itemcget` gives `#000000`.
- A rectangle and a circle are one box quad each. An ellipse, an arc, a polygon, a line, and a dashed outline go through the coverage rows of `path.zig` with 4 by 4 samples in each pixel. A line with an odd width is moved by half a pixel, so that a line on whole coordinates covers whole pixels, as on X11. A smooth line uses the curve of `TkMakeBezierCurve`. A miter join is drawn as a bevel.
- `bbox` follows the rules of Tk for rectangles, ovals, arcs, lines with arrows, polygons, smooth lines, images, and bitmaps, and the values of the test file equal `wish`. A text item has the size of the font of ztk, so its box differs from `wish`.
- `find closest` and `find overlapping` use the distance to the shape for rectangles, ovals, arcs, lines, and polygons, and the bounding box for the other types. `find overlapping` tests the distance from the center of the area, so a thin shape that crosses a corner of a large area can be missed.
- The current item is the top item under the pointer within `-closeenough` that is not hidden or disabled. The current item does not change while a button is down, as in Tk. A change of the items under a pointer that does not move picks no new current item until the next motion.
- `-confine 0` has no effect: the view stays within the scroll region. With no scroll region, a scroll moves the view with no limit and `xview` gives `0.0 1.0`, as in `wish`. `xview moveto` and a scroll by units or pages ease as in task 07. With an increment, the left or top edge of the window goes to a multiple of the increment, as in Tk.
- A window item accepts a child of the canvas. A window that is not a child gives `can't use .x in a window item of this canvas`, where `wish` accepts a descendant of an ancestor of the canvas. During a smooth scroll a window item follows the view at the next layout.
- The stand-in `image` command has `create photo` with `-file` (PNG only, from the folder of the script), `-data` (base64 PNG), `-width`, and `-height`, and `delete`, `names`, `width`, `height`, `type`, `types`, and `inuse` (always 0). A photo has `blank`, `cget`, `configure`, `get`, and `put` (a color with `-to`, or rows of colors). An image is drawn as one box quad for each run of equal pixels in a row, so a large photo gives many quads.
- `ztk::animate {.c tagOrId} -coords {...} -fill red` animates `-coords` and the color and number options of each item that matches. The render thread moves an item while the UI thread is busy when the paint now and the paint at the end have the same count of quads: a rectangle, a circle, a text, an image, or a bitmap. The other items move on the UI thread.
- A drag of an item runs its `<B1-Motion>` binding on the UI thread, and the moved item shows in the next frame. The render thread does not move a canvas item with the pointer by itself.
- A smooth scroll of the canvas moves on the UI thread, because the items in the window at the start and at the end of a move differ.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-25, development machine with a load average of 1 to 3: `canvas-10000-scroll` (a canvas of 10 000 items, 5 000 cells of a rectangle and a text, scrolls by fine wheel steps, one step for each frame, with transitions on) median 1.05 ms and 99th percentile 1.52 ms; `canvas-drag` (the pointer drags one item of the same canvas through its `<B1-Motion>` binding, one motion for each frame) median 1.10 ms and 99th percentile 1.56 ms. Both are under the gate of 8 ms.
- The Tk test files do not run in ztk: the interpreter has no `package` command, so `tcltest` does not load. `wish` 8.6.16 under `xvfb-run` passes `canvas.test` 135 of 137 (2 skipped), `canvImg.test` 11 of 84 (73 skipped), `canvMoveto.test` 8 of 8, `canvPs.test` 10 of 11 (1 skipped), `canvRect.test` 51 of 52 (1 skipped), `canvText.test` 89 of 98 (9 skipped), and `canvWind.test` 5 of 5, with the wrapper of `tools/tk-run.sh`.

## The canvas apps (task 14b)

- `examples/spreadsheet.tcl` and `examples/vector.tcl` use only the widgets of this point of the stage. A menu, a list, a notebook, and a text widget come in tasks 11, 12, 13, and 15, and those tasks change the apps. The console of the vector tool shows its last 3 lines in a label, and its history is in the entry (Up and Down).
- The interpreter has no `package` command, and the `ztk` program has no file access by default (D19). Save and Load of both apps keep the text in a variable when the script has no `open` command, and write `sheet.csv`, `drawing.svg`, and `drawing.ztv` in the current folder when it has one (in `wish`). The page of the web build has no download of the file yet.
- The spreadsheet has the columns A to Z and 10 000 rows. A formula is an `expr` expression after `=`, with cell names and ranges. A division gives a fraction. Copy and paste keep the text of a formula, and a pasted formula reads the same cells. Undo covers the cell text, and the number formats and the column widths are not in it. With no `clipboard` command, Copy keeps the text in the app.
- The canvas has no alpha channel, so the opacity of a shape mixes its colors with the white of the page. The SVG export writes the real `opacity`. A text item does not turn (`-angle` has no effect), so the rotation of a text shape moves its position only. A resize of a rotated rectangle or ellipse scales its unrotated box. The zoom scales the items with `scale`, so the stroke widths and the text keep their size on the screen.
- The canvas draws an ellipse that is not a circle, a polygon, and a line through the path rasterizer on each paint. A drawing with 5 000 such ellipses of 5 by 3 units took a median of 78.6 ms and a 99th percentile of 154.8 ms for each frame of a pan (`zig build bench`, an earlier form of `vector-10000-pan` that moved the items, 2026-11-25). The gate case has rectangles and circles, which are one box quad each, and a pan moves the view and not the items.
- The scripts of the console run in a child interpreter (`interp create con`) with the aliases `shape`, `select`, `move`, `clear`, and `puts`, and a proc `each`. The `interp` of ztcl gives a child the step limit and the memory limit of its caller, and a script cannot set them for a child. The new `ztk::limit` sets them for one script: `ztk::limit -steps 200000 -memory 16000000 {interp eval con $script}`. In `wish` the app uses `interp limit` with a command limit and a time limit of 2 s.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-25, development machine: `sheet-10000-scroll` (the spreadsheet with 10 000 rows of numbers and a formula in each row, fine wheel steps, one step for each frame, with transitions on) median 0.75 ms and 99th percentile 5.20 ms; `vector-10000-pan` (the vector tool with 10 000 more rectangles and circles, a drag with Space held, one motion for each frame) median 3.30 ms and 99th percentile 3.86 ms; `vector-10000-drag` (a drag of one selected shape of the same drawing, with snap to the grid) median 0.02 ms and 99th percentile 4.80 ms. All are under the gate of 8 ms. The spreadsheet makes the items of a new block of 16 rows on a scroll, and those frames are the slow ones.

## entry additions and spinbox (task 08)

- `entry` and `spinbox` have the options of `wish` 8.6.16 with their defaults. `-cursor`, `-buttoncursor`, `-exportselection`, `-highlightbackground`, `-highlightcolor`, `-highlightthickness`, `-insertborderwidth`, `-selectborderwidth`, `-activebackground`, `-buttonbackground`, `-buttondownrelief`, and `-buttonuprelief` are stored and have no effect on the look. `-disabledbackground`, `-disabledforeground`, `-readonlybackground`, `-insertbackground`, `-selectbackground`, and `-selectforeground` change the look only when the script sets them. Otherwise the theme gives the colors.
- The validation follows `EntryValidateChange` and `EntrySetValue` of Tk: the modes, the `%` fields with the quotes of Tk (backslashes, no braces), `-invalidcommand`, the forced validation of a variable and of a spinbox step, `validate` in the mode `all`, and `-validate none` after an error, a result that is not a boolean, or a loop. The error goes to `bgerror` without the line of `errorInfo` that Tk adds.
- `tk windowingsystem` is `ztk`, so `<<Paste>>` deletes the selection before it inserts, as the bindings of Tk do on Windows and macOS. `wish` on X11 keeps the selection.
- The `clipboard` command has `append`, `clear`, and `get` with their options and messages. The clipboard is local to the App: the Wayland and browser backends do not connect it to the clipboard of the system yet. The `selection` command (the PRIMARY selection) is not in ztk.
- The class bindings bind Control-x, Control-c, Control-v, Control-z, Control-Shift-z, Control-y, Control-slash, and Control-backslash to `event generate` of the virtual events, because `event add` is not in ztk. The bindings of the virtual events (`<<Cut>>`, `<<Copy>>`, `<<Paste>>`, `<<Clear>>`, `<<SelectAll>>`, `<<SelectNone>>`) do what the bindings of Tk do. A spinbox binds `<Key-Up>` and `<Key-Down>` beside `<<PrevLine>>` and `<<NextLine>>`.
- `bbox` and `scan` count logical pixels with the font of ztk, so `bbox` differs from `wish`. `scan dragto` moves the view 10 times the drag in pixels, where Tk counts the drag in average character widths.
- A spinbox has `-format` with `format` of the interpreter, and the format of Tk from the range and the increment when `-format` is empty. A press on a button invokes it on the release, or after `-repeatdelay` and then each `-repeatinterval` while the button is held, as in Tk. `identify` gives an empty string for a spinbox that has no size yet, where `wish` gives the elements of a window of one pixel.
- The modern theme draws the buttons of a spinbox as a column with two chevrons at the right edge (parts `::buttonup` and `::buttondown`). Each button has its own hover and pressed state and transition.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-25, development machine with a load average of 2 to 6: `type-entry` (one key in an entry) median 0.028 ms and 99th percentile 0.036 ms, under the gate of 8 ms. In the same run `scroll-wheel` had a 99th percentile of 4.71 ms.

## ttk::style and the simple ttk widgets (task 09)

- `ttk::button`, `ttk::checkbutton`, `ttk::radiobutton`, `ttk::label`, `ttk::frame`, `ttk::labelframe`, `ttk::entry`, `ttk::separator`, and `ttk::sizegrip` have the options of `wish` 8.6.16 in its order, with its defaults and messages. Each class builds on the code of its classic class, and the options of the classic class that `ttk` does not have are hidden: `configure` does not list them, and a script that names one gets `unknown option`.
- `ttk::style configure`, `map`, `lookup`, `layout`, `element create`, `element names`, `element options`, `theme create`, `theme names`, `theme settings`, and `theme use` have the messages of `wish`. Each theme starts with the settings and the layouts of the same theme of `wish`, so `lookup` and `configure` give the values of `wish`. Those settings give no CSS rules: the look comes from the theme sheet.
- The settings of a script become rules with the origin `ttk`. A style `Accent.TButton` is the selector `TButton.Accent`, and `.` is a list of the `ttk` types. The options with a CSS form are `-foreground`, `-background` (`-fieldbackground` for an entry), `-bordercolor`, `-borderwidth`, `-relief`, `-padding`, `-font` (a family and a size), and `-indicatorcolor` (the part `::indicator`). The other options are stored, and `lookup` gives them, with no effect on the look. A layout that a script sets is stored and has no effect.
- `ttk::style map` gives one rule for each pair, in reverse order, so that the first pair that matches wins as in Tk. A pair with more states has a higher specificity, so it wins over an earlier pair with fewer states, where Tk takes the earlier pair.
- The themes `default`, `clam`, and `alt` have the modern look, and `classic` has the look of Tk on X11. `ztk::style theme modern` selects `default`, and `ztk::style theme classic` selects `classic`. A theme of `theme create` has the look of its parent.
- `active` and `hover` share one state flag of the widget, so `state active` sets both. The pointer sets both, as in `wish`.
- `identify` gives `border`, `padding`, or the element of the content (`label`, `textarea`, `border`, `separator`, or `sizegrip`) from the box of the widget. `wish` gives the element of its layout, for example `focus` at the edge of a button.
- `-state` of a `ttk` widget accepts any word, as in `wish`: `disabled` and `readonly` set the state flag, and other words clear it. `-width` of a button or a label is a string: a positive number gives the width of the text in characters, and a negative number a minimum. `-underline` is stored and has no effect. `-image` with `-compound` works for `ttk::button` and `ttk::label`, and the image of a checkbutton or a radiobutton is stored with no effect.
- `ttk::labelframe -labelanchor` gives the message of the classic `labelframe` for a bad value. `wish` gives `Bad label anchor specification`.
- A `ttk::checkbutton` and a `ttk::radiobutton` leave a variable that does not exist as it is, and show the state `alternate`, as in `wish`.
- The classic theme draws the reliefs of Tk with the shades of `TkpGetShadows` on square corners. A round shape (a radiobutton indicator) has a flat border. The classic scrollbar has no arrows.
- The size grip resizes its toplevel with `wm geometry` from a drag. No test drives the grip in the web build.
- The Tk test files of these widgets do not run in ztk: the interpreter has no `package` command, so `tcltest` does not load. `wish` 8.6.16 under `xvfb-run` passes `ttk/ttk.test` 97 of 97, `ttk/checkbutton.test` 8 of 8, `ttk/radiobutton.test` 8 of 8, `ttk/labelframe.test` 19 of 19, `ttk/entry.test` 37 of 37, `ttk/validate.test` 26 of 32 (6 skipped), `ttk/layout.test` 2 of 2, and `ttk/image.test` 5 of 5, with the wrapper of `tools/tk-run.sh`.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-25, development machine with a load average of 1.6 to 3.6: `ttk-hover-1000` (1 000 `ttk::label` and `ttk::button` widgets, a hover color from `ttk::style map`, the pointer moves to another button each 10 frames) median 0.87 ms and 99th percentile 1.04 ms, under the gate of 8 ms.

## ttk range widgets (task 10)

- `ttk::combobox`, `ttk::spinbox`, `ttk::scale`, `ttk::scrollbar`, and `ttk::progressbar` have the options of `wish` 8.6.16 in its order, with its defaults and messages. The combobox builds on the entry, and the spin box, the scale, and the scrollbar on their classic classes, with the options of the classic class hidden. A double option of a `ttk` widget keeps the text of the script (`-value 120` gives `120`), as in `wish`.
- The class bindings are those of the classic classes and of the entry, with the keys and the pointer of the list of a combobox. The lists of `bind TCombobox` and the others differ from `wish`. The combobox has no `ComboboxListbox` and `ComboboxPopdown` bind tags, and the wheel on a closed combobox scrolls the text where `wish` steps the value.
- The list of a combobox is a popover that the toplevel draws over its widgets, with the grab on the combobox while it is open. `wish` makes a toplevel `$cb.popdown` with a listbox. The list starts on the current value, or on no row. A combobox with an empty `-values` does not open its list. `-height` of 0 or less shows 10 rows.
- `ttk::scale set` runs `-command` and gives an empty result. `wish` gives the result of `-command`. `identify` gives `slider` or `trough`, where `wish` gives the element of its layout.
- `ttk::scrollbar identify` gives `thumb` or `trough`: the modern scrollbar has no arrows. `delta` and `fraction` count from the ends of the trough.
- `ttk::spinbox -command` runs with no `%` substitution, as in `wish`. A change of `-values`, `-from`, or `-to` keeps the text. `identify` gives `uparrow`, `downarrow`, or `textarea`.
- `ttk::progressbar` draws the indeterminate bar from the value when `start` does not run, and moves it back and forth in 1.2 s on the render thread while `start` runs. `-phase` is stored with no effect. `identify` gives `pbar` inside the widget.
- A `ttk::entry`, `ttk::combobox`, or `ttk::spinbox` does not create a `-textvariable` that does not exist, as in `wish`.
- The requested sizes come from the font and the theme. `wish` gives 100 by 19 for a horizontal progress bar and 177 for the width of a combobox. ztk gives 100 by 14 and 213.
- The Tk test files of these widgets (`ttk/combobox.test`, `ttk/spinbox.test`, `ttk/scale.test`, `ttk/progressbar.test`) do not run in ztk: the interpreter has no `package` command. The counts of `wish` were not measured for this task.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-25, development machine, load not measured: `progress-indeterminate` (an indeterminate bar with `start` beside 1,000 widgets, one frame each 16 ms) median 0.005 ms and 99th percentile 0.022 ms; `combobox-open` (the list of 40 values opens beside 1,000 widgets) median 1.12 ms and 99th percentile 2.22 ms. Both are under the gate of 8 ms.

## listbox (task 11)

- `listbox` has the options of `wish` 8.6.16 in its order, with its defaults and messages, then `-emptytext`. Each subcommand of `listbox.n` gives the results and the messages of `wish` for the cases of `src/tests/listbox.zig`, which were checked against `wish` 8.6.16 under `xvfb-run`.
- An index is `active`, `anchor`, `end`, `@x,y`, or an integer, as in `wish` 8.6.16. `end-1` is a bad index there too.
- A disabled listbox ignores `insert`, `delete`, `activate`, and `selection set`, `clear`, and `anchor`, as in `wish`.
- The class bindings do the selection of the four modes with the steps of the manual page. ztk has no `event add`, so the keys bind to `event generate` of the virtual events, and `bind Listbox` lists more patterns than `wish`. `<B1-Leave>` scrolls one row for each motion event above or below the rows, where `wish` repeats a scroll with a timer. `<<Copy>>` copies the selected rows, one on each line, to the clipboard of the app.
- `-exportselection`, `-setgrid`, `-highlightcolor`, `-highlightbackground`, and `-highlightthickness` are stored with no effect. The theme draws the focus ring.
- The rows have one height: the line of the font, the padding of `::item`, and two times `-selectborderwidth`. `scan dragto` scrolls by pixels. The `-listvariable` link has a write trace only: an `unset` of the variable leaves the rows.
- The selection background of a single selected row eases to the next single selected row in 180 ms, on the render thread when the view does not scroll.
- The listbox has no built-in overlay scrollbar. A script packs a `scrollbar` beside it, as in Tk.
- The Tk test file `listbox.test` does not run in ztk: the interpreter has no `package` command. Its counts in `wish` were not measured for this task.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-25, development machine with a load average of 1.5: `listbox-scroll-10000` (fine wheel steps scroll a listbox of 10,000 rows, one step each 16 ms, and a press selects a row each 20 frames) median 0.056 ms and 99th percentile 0.081 ms, under the gate of 8 ms.

## menu (task 12)

- `menu` has the options of `wish` 8.6.16 in its order, with its defaults and messages. Each entry type has the entry options of `wish` in the order of `entryconfigure`. The cases of `src/tests/menu.zig` were checked against `wish` 8.6.16 under `xvfb-run` on 2026-11-25.
- An index is `active`, `end`, `last`, `none`, `@y`, an integer, or a pattern of a label, as in `wish`. `@y` and `yposition` use the rows of the theme, so their numbers differ from `wish`. `xposition` gives the left padding of the menu.
- The tearoff entry is stored as entry 0, and it cannot be deleted, as in `wish`. A tear-off window does not exist: `invoke` of the tearoff entry has no effect, and `-tearoffcommand` does not run.
- `clone` makes a menu with the options and the entries of the menu. The clone has no link to the menu, and a later change of the menu does not change the clone.
- `activate` does not send `<<MenuSelect>>`, as in `wish`. The pointer and the keys of the drawn menus send it (PR 3 of `docs/dev/menus.md`). A native menu sends it through the menu handler.
- The drawn menus are not in this PR. Without native menus, `post` and `postcascade` record the state, and the menu does not show until PR 3.
- `. configure` has the options of `toplevel`. `. cget -class` gives `Toplevel`, where `wish` gives the class of the application.

## panedwindow and ttk::panedwindow (task 13)

- `panedwindow` has the options of `wish` 8.6.16 in its order, with its defaults and messages. `-proxyrelief` has the default `flat`, where `wish` gives `{}` as the default and `flat` as the value. Each pane option of `paneconfigure` gives the five words of `wish`. `-minsize`, `-padx`, and `-pady` show the number in logical pixels, also when the script gave a unit such as `1c`.
- `panecget` with an unknown option gives `not managed by this window`, as in `wish`.
- The sizes along the axis, the stretch, `sash coord`, `sash place`, `sash mark`, `sash dragto`, `identify`, and the proxy give the numbers of `wish` for the cases of `src/tests/paned.zig`. One case differs: when no pane can stretch and the widget is smaller than its panes, `wish` moves the last pane to the end of the widget over the pane before it, and ztk cuts the last pane at the end of the widget.
- A change of `-orient` drops the sizes of the sash moves. `wish` keeps them, and its requested width after the change was not matched.
- `ttk::panedwindow` gives the sizes, the weights, and the `sashpos` pushes of `wish` for the cases of the tests. The rest of a share goes to the first weighted panes one pixel at a time, or from the last weighted panes for a smaller widget, as `wish` gives it. Its sash is 5 logical pixels, the size of the default theme of `wish`. `identify element` gives `hsash` or `vsash`.
- The class bindings are in Zig (`::ztk::priv::paned`), so `bind Panedwindow` and `bind TPanedwindow` list their patterns in another order than `wish`. `TPanedwindow` has no `<Enter>` binding.
- Over a sash the pointer has the shape of `-sashcursor`, else a resize shape, with no change of `-cursor`. `wish` sets `-cursor` of the widget in its binding.
- `-sashrelief`, `-proxyrelief`, `-proxyborderwidth`, and `-proxybackground` are stored with no effect. The theme draws the sash (`::sash`, `::sash-active`) and the proxy (`::sash-active`).
- The Tk test file `panedwindow.test` does not run in ztk: the interpreter has no `package` command. Its counts in `wish` were not measured for this task.
- `zig build bench`, ReleaseFast, 2026-11-25, development machine: `sash-drag` (a drag of the sash of a `panedwindow` with 500 labels in each pane, one motion for each frame) has a median of 0.88 ms and a 99th percentile of 1.00 ms. The gate is 8 ms.

## ttk::notebook (task 13)

- `ttk::notebook` has the options of `wish` 8.6.16 in its order. Each subcommand gives the results and the messages of `wish` for the cases of `src/tests/notebook.zig`, also `index end`, `index current`, and `index @x,y`. A hidden tab that `select` names becomes `normal`, and `select` of a disabled tab keeps the selection, as in `wish`.
